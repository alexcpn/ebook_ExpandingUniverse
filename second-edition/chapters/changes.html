Changes from the First Edition

Changes from the First Edition

The first edition (1998) is preserved unchanged. This page lists what this edition changes, so that the two can be compared.

Corrections of fact

Chapter First edition Second edition
2 Cepheid luminosity is "directly proportional" to period, L ∝ P Luminosity grows as a power of the period; the Leavitt law is linear in log P: M_V = −2.43 (log P − 1) − 4.05
2 Moving-cluster method described only as a change in angular size Explained with the convergent point and the formula d = v_r tan θ / (4.74 μ); the angular-size picture kept as an equivalent view
2 Parallax works "up to 300 light years" True for ground-based work in 1998; Gaia now reaches thousands of parsecs
3 Distant galaxies "receding fastest at 1200 km/s" Hubble's 1929 sample reached about 1 000 km/s; by 1931 Humason had measured nearly 20 000 km/s
3 Humason, 1933, cluster in Ursa Major Date and object could not be confirmed; replaced with the documented Hubble & Humason (1931) result
3 Credit for the expansion to Hubble alone Slipher's redshifts and Lemaître's 1927 derivation acknowledged; IAU's 2018 name "Hubble–Lemaître law" noted
6 Gamow proposed that "many of the heavy elements" were made in the Big Bang Correct as history, but the idea failed: no stable mass-5 or mass-8 nuclei, so only light elements are made; heavy elements come from stars
6 Prediction attributed to Gamow only Alpher & Herman's 1948 prediction of ≈ 5 K added
7 "Dickie" Dicke
7 "Excess noise in their sources" Excess noise in their antenna
7 "The discovery … was a direct prediction of the Big Bang theory" The CMB was a prediction; its discovery confirmed it

8 The primordial state "exploded in a big bang" The Big Bang was the expansion of space everywhere, not an explosion from a point into space

9 Stars begin with 20–30% helium Primordial helium now measured as Y = 0.245 ± 0.003

10 "In the beginning of the 19th century, scientists were confident that by the end of the century…" The well-known overconfidence was at the end of the 19th century

Figures

  • All scanned figures from published textbooks have been removed. Besides avoiding copyright problems, several of them illustrated a different topic from the text around them (galaxy photographs in the nucleosynthesis chapter; star-cluster photographs in the hot Big Bang chapter). Some image files combined two unrelated figures.
  • 26 new diagrams were drawn from physical formulae or published, cited values. They are generated by scripts/make_figures.py.
  • Figure numbering is now consistent within each chapter (Figure 2.1, 2.2, …). The first edition mixed its own numbering with the source textbooks' numbering.
  • Where a figure uses illustrative rather than real data points, its caption says so.

Additions

  • Chapter 1: the light-gathering and resolution formulae; detectors; spectroscopy; radio and space astronomy.
  • Chapter 2: the distance ladder; the distance modulus; the parsec; Leavitt's discovery; Baade's 1952 revision.
  • Chapter 3: the definition of redshift; what uniform expansion means; a numerical value for the Hubble time; how the true age differs from 1/H₀; modern redshift surveys.
  • Chapter 4: the steady-state theory explained; why source counts test it; quasars.
  • Chapter 5: the history of the paradox and why finite age is the main resolution.
  • Chapter 7: COBE and the precise blackbody spectrum; the CMB anisotropies.
  • Chapter 8: the thermal history of the Universe; what the Big Bang does and does not mean.
  • Chapter 9: freeze-out, the deuterium bottleneck, the other light elements, and the lithium problem.
  • Chapter 11 (new): the accelerating Universe, precision cosmology, dark matter, the Hubble tension, new observational windows and inflation.

Style

  • Gendered language ("man", "men of the future") replaced with neutral equivalents. The meaning and the closing image of the conclusion are kept.
  • Equations are written consistently in Unicode, with symbols defined where they are first used.
