Chapter 10: Conclusion

Chapter 10: Conclusion

We can only imagine between the extremes of the tiniest and the vast. But the timescales and distances we come across in our scientific search are either too tiny or too vast to imagine directly. A proton is 10⁻¹⁵ m across; the observable Universe is 10²⁶ m. Nucleosynthesis was over in minutes; the Universe is 13.8 billion years old. Yet the truth ingrained in the heart of the atom is the same truth embedded in the stars. The helium in the Sun was made by the same nuclear physics that we test in laboratories, in the first three minutes of cosmic history.

The story in this book is a story of a ladder. Parallax gave distances to the nearest stars. The H-R diagram and star clusters extended them across the Galaxy. Cepheids carried the measurement to other galaxies, and there Hubble found the expansion. From the expansion came the idea of a beginning. From the beginning came three predictions: the Universe should evolve, it should be filled with cool blackbody radiation, and it should be one-quarter helium. All three have been confirmed.

It has been only about 10 000 years since the first farmers began to settle. At the end of the 19th century some physicists were confident that the great work of physics was almost finished, and that only the details remained to be filled in. Within a few decades relativity and quantum mechanics had overturned that view, and the expanding Universe had been discovered. In the same way, cosmology has repeatedly found mysteries where it expected answers. In 1998 these were the "missing mass" and the large-scale structure of the Universe. Since then they have been joined by the discovery that the expansion is accelerating, driven by a dark energy that no current theory explains (Chapter 11).

The more we learn, the more we find there is to learn. It may be a very long time before questions like "What is matter?", "What is light?" and "What is gravity?" are answered in their full sense. To those people of the future we may seem like the first farmers of science, just beginning to settle near the banks of the starry, eternal rivers of truth.
